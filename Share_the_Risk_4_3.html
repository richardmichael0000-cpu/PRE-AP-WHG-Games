Share the Risk

Merchants, voyages and joint-stock companies, c.1600

Merchants, ships and risk

  1. Write the teams on the whiteboard. Every team starts with ƒ100 (100 guilders).
  2. Sail alone. Each team chooses one voyage it can afford, pays the cost and rolls the die. The further the voyage, the bigger the prize, and the bigger the danger.
  3. Play a second round if there is time. A team with no money left cannot sail.
  4. The company, 1602. Every team puts all its money into the VOC. Teams with nothing left become the company's captains and launch its fleet.
  5. Share out what the fleet brings home.
A joint-stock company
Many people buy shares. Together they pay for many ships. They share the profits, and they share the losses.

The money and the odds are invented for the game. The routes, goods and dangers are real.

1602: the VOC

Every team puts all its money into the Dutch East India Company (VOC). Hundreds of other people in Amsterdam buy shares too. Together they pay for a fleet of at least 15 ships to the Spice Islands, at ƒ100 a ship.

Ask the class

  1. The Spice Islands paid the most. Why didn't every team choose them?
  2. In the company, most ships were lost. Why did no team lose everything?
  3. No team could pay for 15 ships. How did selling shares solve that problem? Why did that matter for voyages to Asia?
  4. Source 6: who paid for the English company's voyages, and what powers did the Queen give it? The full charter also gave it the only right to English trade east of the Cape of Good Hope for 15 years. Why would she agree?
  5. Then and now: what is the same, and what is different, when someone buys shares on Vietnam's stock exchange today?

Joint-stock companies were not new in 1600: England's Muscovy Company dates from 1555. The East India companies used the idea on a far bigger scale. The English company (1600) at first raised money for one voyage at a time; the VOC (1602) joined six city companies and kept its money for years, and its shares were bought and sold in Amsterdam. These companies also used force: Source 6 lets the English company make laws and punish offenders.
